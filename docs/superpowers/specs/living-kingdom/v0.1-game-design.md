# Princess Pulverizer: The Living Kingdom — Version 0.1

Status: **Approved direction, implementation handoff**. Date: 2026-10-18 (PT).
Audience: advanced third-grade reader, roughly age 8–10; suitable to share with younger readers with optional help.
Goal: **a finished playable Chapter One with an expandable engine**, not a linear book or an empty roguelike framework. Target approximately 60–70% of the long-term mechanics in functioning, testable form, with 25–30% of envisioned story content. Those percentages describe project strategy, not an excuse to leave key screens as placeholders.

## Premise and voice

Princess Pulverizer (Princess Serena), with Lucas and Dribble, travels through a persistent, colorful storybook kingdom on her Quest of Kindness. She wants to become a knight, but becoming worthy of the title demands more than conquering monsters. Players must weigh competing obligations and incomplete evidence; reasonable characters disagree. Humor, whimsy, discovery, and adventurous moments balance moral weight.

**Original fan-game content**: draw on the premise and characters of Nancy Krulik's Princess Pulverizer series; do not quote passages from the novels, copy their illustrations, or claim official affiliation. For private prototype use. If later distributed broadly, revisit rights or replace with original characters/world.

The game should treat children as capable readers and thinkers. Do not give a moral numeric score, label every option good/bad, secretly grade virtue, or make "always choose compromise" the winning strategy. Choices must have understandable, proportionate consequences and occasionally no universally satisfying solution.

## Core play

- Persistent kingdom generated once per profile with **seeded layout and scenario variations**. Places, relationships, past choices, items, promises and world-day history persist. **Do not randomly regenerate the whole world on each session.**
- **Session 15–20 minutes** is a target, not a clock-enforced quest step. Chapters span multiple sessions. User can stop at any time; resume exact scene/page/choices. Respect Porthole shell's parent-configured rest and daily cap (current default 25 minutes/day).
- Chapter 1, **Trouble at Willowbrook**, 3–5 exploratory sessions, with bridge, mill, missing supplies and river mystery; 6 locations, recurring NPCs, three interwoven quest tracks, 2+ meaningful delayed consequences, 3 distinct major resolution types plus several variations. The chapter should resolve with a sense of accomplishment yet leave an interesting hook for Chapter 2.
- Three character capacities: **Courage, Ingenuity, Insight** (bounded progression, opportunities rather than binary locks); companions Lucas (observation/caution) and Dribble (culinary/dragon knowledge, warmth/fire, unusual thinking). No health points, combat grind, inventory loot treadmill or XP grinding in V0.1.
- Small useful inventory (e.g., six tangible items), plus **unlimited journal facts/promises** (do not fill inventory with necessary clues). Items unlock options or solve resource problems and should sometimes carry tradeoffs.
- An in-game calendar advances through meaningful actions/travel/rest; **no real-time lockout** and no punishment for days away. State changes are authored and scheduled. The world genuinely changes (different scenes, dialogue and resources) rather than simply displaying a new day number.
- Replay value from route choices, world seed, rotating eligible encounters, mutually incompatible commitments and delayed consequences. Fixed underlying narrative causality; no generated nonsense or unsupervised generative text. A campaign remains deterministic for a given seed + action sequence.
- The main quest is to work toward eight good deeds; Chapter One can award or recognize **a deed earned through meaningful effort**, without equating a specific ideological choice with a correct answer. Keeping a promise, owning mistakes or preserving a relationship can contribute even if a resolution is imperfect.

## Hardware / repository facts (checked October 18)

- Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-2.1; round **480×480** display, native RGB565 rendering, single-finger capacitive touch; ESP32-S3, 8 MB PSRAM / 16 MB flash. Both 160×160 indexed art and 480×480 native scenes exist; text-heavy reading should **prefer readable native fonts** and round-safe clipping, and not force every paragraph into a low-resolution 8px bitmap font.
- Porthole existing launcher has **seven apps and MAX_APPS=8** on this branch. Add as game in `apps/porthole/games/living-kingdom/`; integrate into existing launcher, shell, host simulator and browser emulator. Do not create another `apps/<app>`.
- Respect `AGENTS.md`, `apps/porthole/AGENTS.md`, `docs/hardware.md`, app-local conventions; game-specific `AGENTS.md` required. The shell owns profiles, rest / daily caps, save process and exit. NVS save must be compatible with future appending/version migration and must preserve each child's own campaign.
- Finger-size touch targets at least approximately 72x72 physical pixels, fully inside circular bezel. Do not put tappable arrows at clipped perimeter. Critical text must fit the shrinking circular chord, not just the underlying square.
- Touch only; **no tilt mechanic** is required for reading or choices. Optional swipe gestures may complement but never replace visible buttons.
- Buzzer is harsh; operate silently by default and honor system mute.
- Long narrative passages have a RAM / flash footprint: use compact authored content representation, with stable IDs and no duplicated giant images. Avoid network dependency.

## Reading and decision UX — an explicit requirement

The primary reading interaction is **stable pagination** (rather than uncontrolled momentum scrolling), with visibly tappable **Back** and **Next** controls plus `1/3` progress dots or page number. A long passage may occupy 2–4 pages; readers may freely move backward and forward over **all read pages of the current scene**. An alternate vertical scroll implementation is allowed ONLY if the same re-reading behavior, no unexpected jump-to-end, clear progress and touch ergonomics are demonstrated.

**No story branch commits while reading.** Decision options appear after the last narrative page on a distinct choice screen; that screen has a prominent **RE-READ STORY** action returning to the first page with preserved choice context, and navigation back to the choices without forcing another state change. Optionally allow browsing the party's past dialogue via journal/history. Rereading must not replay effects, trigger new random outcomes, advance day, or mutate NPC state.

Only **confirming a choice** applies effects exactly once. If an action has an irreversible or high-stakes implication, preview stakes in story; no trap choices that cannot be inferred from text. No "confirm every tap" friction, but consider a brief confirm screen for especially consequential decisions with a "read again" path. Back/exit never changes the choice or undoes already committed outcomes; provide subsequent opportunities for repair, not arbitrary undo.

A reading screen:
- occupies the circle's safe center; a scene title and small optional visual vignette occupy top area;
- body is high-contrast dark text on pale parchment or cream text on deep navy, with generous leading and a legible type size tested in simulated **physical 2.1-inch** presentation;
- honors natural English paragraph breaks; page breaks only between sentences or at sensible paragraph boundaries;
- provides small safe-area Back/Next navigation at the bottom and optional portrait/name at top; **long text is welcome** (several pages before deciding).
The last page shows **MAKE A CHOICE**; the choice page displays up to 3 large choices per page, with extra choices paginated instead of tiny targets. Show short option label plus a clarifying subtitle. Accessible return to prior narrative. Distinguish disabled/locked options with **why** only when information is known; don't spoil undiscovered secrets.

The launcher name must be short enough to fit 8-slot layout (e.g., "Kingdom"). Inside the game, use the full title.

## Chapter One narrative scope

Region: Willowbrook village, Old Bridge, Miller's Mill, North Woods, Riverside Marsh, Old Watchtower. Castle/other regions visible on distant map but not traversable until future chapters. NPCs: party (Princess, Lucas, Dribble); Miller Elric, Troll Bram, caretaker Mira, woodworker Tilda, trader Nell, marsh guardian Fen, village child Pippa. Define distinctive motives and memory; avoid reducing the troll to a villain or assigning intent without evidence.

Three interlocking threads:
1. **Broken Bridge**: a troll blocks passage; bridge stones were damaged, but not necessarily by the troll. Investigation yields clues to water and old repairs.
2. **Missing Supplies**: the village has limited flour / grain; the trader, miller and families have conflicting needs. Choosing how to allocate resources affects trust and future options.
3. **River's Secret**: a change in water flow threatens milling and marsh nesting grounds. Repairing one problem shifts costs elsewhere. An alternative channel may need labor and a genuine promise, not magical free-win.

Critical choice: preserve water for the mill, protect the marsh, negotiate imperfect rationing, or invest in a costly shared infrastructure plan. Depending on investigated facts, companion advice, relationships, and supplies, some options vary or unlock. No single maximal outcome. The chosen approach impacts Willowbrook, Bram, the miller, and Fen over subsequent world-days and can be revised with effort.

The chapter must have real authored prose; see `chapter-01-story.md`. Don't ship story summarized into one sentence and then ask players to choose. Narrative pages should support reading for an advanced third grader (dry wit, charming character conflicts, clues and narrative setup).
Content engineering examples and object IDs are in `chapter-01-world.json`, plus data contract in `content-contract.md`. These are **design seed data**, not a preapproved runtime schema; Codex should implement a schema that satisfies the behavior and document differences.

## Persistent systems (V0.1)

**Profile save:** chapter, stable world seed, day, location, scene node ID, read-page index, seen scene IDs and choice history, active/finished quests, clue set, item inventory, capability ranks, NPC relations, promises, scheduled events, chapter outcome, and deterministic generator state. Keep fixed-size/versioned persisted representation; no repeated huge story strings in NVS. If the shell uses only 1 slot for game save, design an efficient snapshot and migration strategy preserving progress as new chapters/IDs are added.

**Quests:** not just boolean. Support available / active / blocked / completed / deferred states and conditional objectives; past event references survive releases. Journal separates known facts from inferences and lists unfinished promises and their original date/context.

**Story engine:** location/scenario/encounter IDs, preconditions, multi-page text, option IDs, conditional visibility, atomic effects, delayed scheduled effects, and stable deterministic selection from eligible authored encounters. Repeat handling: don't replay one-off scenes; other visits get time/relationship dependent narrative. Add a lightweight content validator for missing links, duplicate IDs, mutually impossible prerequisites, unreachable outcomes, and unbounded loops.

**Generative variation:** choose at campaign initialization among 2–3 initial facts/rumors/route or NPC variations (seeded). Encounter selection respects current state, prevents repeated identical dialogue, and reserves major story beats for the right time. Never silently choose an encounter that invalidates a main quest; deterministic logs let host tests replay decisions.

**Progression:** growth opportunities gated by sustained decisions and discoveries, not one "correct" moral response; companions add situational insight. Unlock a skill rank during chapter only if earned by an actual accomplishment; avoid a constant arithmetic XP bar.

## Non-goals V0.1

No conventional combat, crafting economy, shop catalog, procedural map of hundreds of rooms, neural text generation on the device, gyroscope controls, multiplayer, real-time online services, huge branching prose tree, arbitrary permadeath, or more than the starter chapter. No pressure to pre-author Chapters 2–8.

## Visual direction

Choose **colorful illustrated pixel-fantasy**, crisp and warm, with a limited rendering budget. Forest greens, river blues, pumpkin golds, red rooftops, cream parchment, deep indigo frame. Small illustrations establish scenes; **the text is primary**. Design variants should borrow richer picturebook warmth rather than stark monochrome terminal. Distinct original game palette, font and art; don't reuse Biscuit/Pets Club assets.

See `starter-screens.svg` for native-layout reference and `ux-flow.md` for interaction details. The generated concept boards in the ChatGPT discussion are additional moodboard references; the original generated PNGs are not bundled in this branch. The committed SVG is the self-contained handoff artwork and must not be mistaken for final production sprites.

## Definition of playable

Player can open Kingdom from the shell, start/resume separate child profiles, explore the starter region, read multi-page prose, review it from the choice screen, make and see consequential decisions, use an item or companion option, complete at least one multi-day chapter resolution, receive a journal update, save and resume through interruption, and visibly revisit changed locations days later. Host/browser emulator demonstrates each path; check all real screen sizes for clipping and page navigation. A narrative/engine test verifies choice atomicity, deterministic generation, save migration and delayed consequences.

Leave the first playtest in a self-contained and appealing state, with Chapter One playable **to a conclusion** and a tasteful "To be continued" hook; do not leave players stuck on a "feature coming soon" page before resolution.
