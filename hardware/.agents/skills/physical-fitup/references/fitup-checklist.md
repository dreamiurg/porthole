## Interpretation

- A constant offset at every point often indicates a member length, plate thickness, or spacer-stack difference.
- A changing offset indicates parallelism, squareness, twist, or a hole-pattern/assembly-order issue.
- A result that changes when fasteners are tightened indicates preload or a datum that is not actually seated.
- A freely moving assembly with a small measured discrepancy may still be acceptable only if the functional tolerance and load path permit it; document the decision rather than hiding the discrepancy.

## Stop conditions

Stop and resolve the basis before final tightening or irreversible work when:

- inside and outside dimensions are being compared;
- a purchased part's drawing conflicts with its model;
- front/rear or top/bottom readings prove nonparallelism;
- a joint would need a bolt to bend a plate, rail, or extrusion;
- the proposed fix changes a load-bearing hole, edge distance, or guide datum.
